Abstract:  Goods and Services Tax (GST) and Sales Tax Input Tax Credit 
(ITC) fraud have become a major concern in modern tax administration due 
to the increasing use of fake invoices, circular trading, shell companies, and 
fraudulent credit claims. These frauds cause substantial revenue leakage and 
weaken the integrity of the taxation system by enabling taxpayers to claim 
illegitimate credits without genuine business transactions. Traditional fraud 
detection methods, which rely heavily on manual auditing, rule-based checks, 
and post-fraud investigation, are often slow, reactive, and insufficient for 
uncovering large-scale hidden fraud networks. In real-world GST ecosystems, 
fraud patterns are not always directly visible in individual transactions; 
instead, they emerge through complex behavioural, temporal, and relational 
patterns spread across multiple invoices, taxpayers, and filing cycles. This 
creates a strong need for an intelligent, scalable, and explainable fraud 
detection framework capable of learning hidden patterns from large volumes 
of tax data. 
To address this challenge, this project proposes a JEPA-LLM Tuning 
Framework for Combating GST and Sales Tax Input Tax Credit Fraud 
Pattern Detection. The framework combines domain-specific GST fraud 
knowledge, self-supervised representation learning, fraud scoring, and 
natural-language explanation to identify suspicious ITC claims more 
effectively. The proposed system is inspired by three major directions: GST 
fraud and forensic accounting analysis, hybrid AI-based financial fraud 
detection, and explainable multi-layer fraud assessment. At the domain level, 
the framework targets major GST/ITC fraud behaviours such as fake invoice 
generation, abnormal input credit claims, shell-entity participation, circular 
trading loops, invoice without actual movement of goods or services, and 
coordinated fraud among linked taxpayers. 
A core contribution of this project is the integration of Joint Embedding 
Predictive Architecture (JEPA) as the primary pattern-learning component. 
In this framework, JEPA is used as a self-supervised representation learning 
module that learns hidden behavioural structures from largely unlabeled GST 
data. Instead of depending only on fully labelled fraud examples, JEPA 
models the normal relationships among invoices, taxpayers, filing history, and 
transaction flows, and then learns compact predictive embeddings that 

represent expected GST behaviour. When a taxpayer, invoice sequence, or 
transaction network deviates significantly from these learned behavioural 
patterns, the system flags it as potentially suspicious. This makes JEPA 
particularly valuable for GST fraud detection because real tax datasets often 
contain very limited confirmed fraud labels, while fraudsters continuously 
change their tactics to avoid rule-based detection. By learning behavioural 
regularities and deviations, JEPA helps capture subtle fraud signatures such 
as unusual spikes in ITC claims, repeated invoice cycles, suspicious buyer–
seller relationships, circular credit movement, and shell-company-linked 
transaction behaviour. 
The proposed framework follows a multi-layer fraud detection strategy by 
combining three categories of features: invoice-level features, taxpayer 
behaviour features, and transaction/network-level features. Invoice-level 
features may include invoice amount, tax value, credit claimed, filing 
frequency, duplicate invoice indicators, and date inconsistencies. Taxpayer 
behaviour features capture patterns such as sudden increases in ITC 
utilization, mismatch between outward and inward tax declarations, refund 
anomalies, and irregular filing habits. Network-level features model buyer–
seller relationships, repeated entity interactions, shared contact information, 
shell-company clusters, and circular trading loops. JEPA encodes these 
heterogeneous patterns into a unified latent representation, which is then 
passed to a fraud scoring module for classification or anomaly detection. 
To enhance interpretability and usability, the framework also integrates LLM 
tuning as an explanation and decision-support layer. Once suspicious entities 
or transactions are identified, the LLM generates human-readable fraud 
summaries, highlighting the probable reasons behind each alert. For example, 
it can explain whether a taxpayer was flagged due to abnormal ITC growth, 
repeated transactions with linked firms, suspicious invoice chains, or 
inconsistent filing behaviour. This improves transparency and makes the 
system more useful for auditors, investigators, and tax officers who require 
understandable evidence rather than raw anomaly scores alone.
