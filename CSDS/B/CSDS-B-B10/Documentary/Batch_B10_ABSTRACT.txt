Title of the project: HYBRID CHAOTIC LIGHT IMAGE 
ENCRYPTION FOR SECURE UAV TRANSMISSION : 
WAVELET AND XOR-DIFFUSION APPROACH 
Abstract: 
          The secure transmission of digital images has become a critical requirement in modern 
UAV communication systems due to the increasing risk of cyber threats targeting sensitive visual 
information. Images transmitted over wireless communication channels are vulnerable to 
interception, modification, statistical attacks, differential attacks, and unauthorized access. 
Existing encryption techniques often provide strong security but may involve high computational 
complexity, making them less suitable for real-time image transmission.  
This project presents a Hybrid Chaotic Lightweight Image Encryption for Secure UAV 
Transmission using a Wavelet and XOR-Diffusion Approach, designed as a purely software-
based encryption framework that combines computational efficiency with robust security. The 
proposed method employs the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to decompose the input image 
into multiple frequency components, enabling efficient image representation while preserving 
essential visual features. A chaotic map is utilized to generate highly unpredictable encryption 
keys, taking advantage of its randomness, nonlinearity, and extreme sensitivity to initial 
conditions.The generated chaotic keys are integrated with an XOR-based diffusion algorithm to 
perform pixel-level encryption, effectively reducing pixel correlation and increasing resistance 
against cryptographic attacks. 
The proposed encryption framework provides multiple security advantages, including a large key 
space, high information entropy, strong key sensitivity, low pixel correlation, and resistance to 
brute-force, statistical, differential, replay, and interception attacks. The performance of the 
proposed approach is evaluated using standard image encryption metrics such as Information 
Entropy, Histogram Analysis, Correlation Coefficient, NPCR (Number of Pixel Change Rate), 
UACI (Unified Average Changing Intensity), PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio), MSE (Mean 
Square Error), Encryption Time, Decryption Time, and Key Sensitivity Analysis.
