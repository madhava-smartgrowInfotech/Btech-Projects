# AquaVision - How it works

## Architecture

```
React + Vite (5214) --/api proxy--> FastAPI (8214) --> services
                                                   |-- twin.py       WNTR / EPANET digital twin
                                                   |-- quality.py    stacking ensemble + SHAP + guideline checks
                                                   |-- demand.py     XGBoost forecaster + Open-Meteo
                                                   |-- leaks.py      RandomForest detector + signature localisation
                                                   |-- imbalance.py  zone supply/demand, equity, what-if search
                                                   |-- anomaly.py    IsolationForest on customer meters
                                                   `-- ops.py        alerts and twin state (active leaks)
                                           SQLite data/app.db: users, quality_checks, leak_events, alerts
```

Consumption and meter data are clearly labelled **sample data**. Each zone's daily demand is the zone's base volume multiplied by factors for day of week, temperature above 30 C, rain, slow growth, and AR(1) noise. Zone 3 (East Hills) is the most sensitive to heat. Meter anomalies are injected with a known ground truth so the detector can be scored.

## F1 - Water quality

- Median imputation, then a `StackingClassifier`: RF (300 trees), XGBoost (300), Gradient Boosting (200) and a Decision Tree (depth 6). 5-fold out-of-fold probabilities feed a logistic-regression meta-learner.
- **SHAP:** an exact Shapley explainer (2^9 coalitions, 40-row background) runs over the full ensemble's potable probability, so the reasons explain the actual prediction, not a single member model.
- **Guideline checks:** pH 6.5-8.5, TDS <= 1000 mg/L, chloramines <= 4 mg/L, sulfate <= 250 mg/L, conductivity <= 400 uS/cm, organic carbon <= 2 mg/L, trihalomethanes <= 80 ug/L, turbidity <= 5 NTU, hardness <= 300 mg/L.
- Every check is stored. A "not potable" result raises a quality alert.

## F2 - Network twin

- A 6x8 grid of junctions 500 m apart, laid over Hyderabad for the map, split into 5 zones.
- The treatment plant (reservoir, 12 m head) feeds the network through PUMP1 into two 450 mm trunk mains. A 28 m service tank sits at the north.
- 350 mm trunk rows, 200/150 mm distribution pipes, Hazen-Williams C = 110.
- Pressure-driven demand (EPANET 2.2 PDA): full service at 20 m, none below 3 m.
- Every junction has a background-leakage emitter (q = C sqrt(p), about 10% of demand at 35 m), so NRW responds to pressure.
- A 24 h extended-period run takes about 0.05 s. The dashboard, map and imbalance views all run the twin with the currently active (injected) leaks.

## F3 - Demand forecast

- Target: demand relative to the zone's 28-day level. Features: zone, weekday, month, the ratios lag1/level, lag7/level and 7-day mean/level, max/min temperature, rain, and the temperature anomaly versus the last 28 days.
- XGBoost (400 trees, depth 3). The forecast is recursive: each predicted day feeds the next day's lags.
- Weather for the forecast window comes from the Open-Meteo forecast API, cached for 3 hours. If it is unreachable, the last cached forecast is used, and failing that a climatology built from the cached history.

## F4 - Leak detection and localisation

- **Observations:** the 03:00 minimum-night-flow window. 16 pressure loggers and 5 zone inlet meters give 21 readings.
- **Residuals** = observed - the twin's no-leak expectation. The "observed" readings are simulated with ±3% zone demand and ±4% node demand uncertainty, plus sensor noise (0.05 m, 0.35 L/s).
- **Detection:** a RandomForest (300 trees) on the 21 residuals, trained on 700 leak (2-20 L/s) and 700 no-leak scenarios.
- **Localisation:** each of the 82 distribution pipes has a sensitivity signature (the noise-free residual of a 10 L/s leak at its midpoint). The observed residual is noise-scaled, the pressure part is mean-centred to remove the pump-head common mode, and pipes are ranked by cosine similarity. Leak size is estimated by projection onto the signature.
- Injecting a leak (engineer role) adds it to the twin state. Every view then shows its effect (higher NRW, zone losses) until "Repair all".

## F5 - Distribution imbalance

- For each zone: demand (required), supply (DMA inflow), delivered volume, losses (emitter outflow), pressure adequacy (share of node-hours at or above 20 m), and average / minimum pressure.
- **Equity score** = 100 x (service level of the worst zone / service level of the best zone), where service level = pressure adequacy x delivered share.
- **Rebalancing:** the twin simulates 11 candidate plans (pump speed 100-115%, optional throttling of a trunk main). It picks the plan with the best `equity - 3 x NRW` and reports the trade-off. What-if runs any setting on demand.

## F6 - Abnormal consumption

- Per meter-day features, each relative to the meter's own baseline (first 14 days, Sundays separate): log daily total, log 01:00-04:00 minimum flow, log peak hour, and night fraction.
- IsolationForest (300 trees, contamination 2.5%).
- Labels: continuous night flow (night flow never drops), burst (a peak far above normal), suspected theft (consumption collapsed), unusual pattern.
- A meter is raised when it shows a named pattern, or an unexplained pattern on 2 or more days, in the last 7 days.

## F7 - Alerts

Alerts are created for leak detections, quality failures, flagged meters and an equity score below 90. They are deduplicated by reference.
