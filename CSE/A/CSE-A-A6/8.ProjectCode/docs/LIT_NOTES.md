## Theme 1 - Base paper and the two supplied companion papers

**[1] Feng and Li, IEEE Access 14:36158-36175, 2026, doi 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3670924. Verified from PDF.** Adaptive Driver Monitoring and Assistance System (ADMAS): MediaPipe Face Mesh (468 landmarks) gives a six-point EAR and a modified four-point EAR-4 (upper eyelid only); head pose (pitch, yaw, roll) by Perspective-n-Point from six landmarks; Whisper-tiny with HTDemucs denoising for voice commands [29]; a Fuzzy Belief Rule-Based inference engine (belief rules after RIMER [30]) fuses EAR-4, head pose and voice intent and triggers a buzzer when the fuzzy output exceeds a risk threshold. A personalised adaptive EAR threshold is set in an initialisation phase using more than 300 frames. Data: four subjects of NTHU-DDD [14], 65,834 frames (29,804 open, 36,030 closed), 80/20 split. Models: Random Forest and XGBoost on EAR versus EAR-4. Reported: EAR-4 recall 89.4 % (abstract; Subject 2, Random Forest, recall 0.8560 -> 0.8940 and F1 0.8130 -> 0.8452 when switching from six-point EAR to EAR-4). Deployed on a Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB (40 TOPS) at 25 FPS. Illustrative EAR threshold in the paper's example trace: 0.18. Limits stated by the authors: the initial split is not subject-independent (subject-independent validation is listed as future work); benchmark data. Target state: drowsiness, fatigue, distraction, not alcohol.

**[2] Baroutian, Aghaei, Ebrahimi Moghaddam, arXiv:2512.04536, Dec 2025. Verified from PDF.** Alcohol-intoxication detection from facial video: facial landmark analysis with a Graph Attention Network (SPIGA landmarks) fused with 3D ResNet spatiotemporal features by a learnable weighted fusion (alpha 0.4704). Data: because many DIF [4] source videos were no longer accessible, the authors curated 202 YouTube videos (101 sober, 101 intoxicated), split into 3,542 shot-level clips (TransNetV2), balanced to 1,771 per class, 80/20 subject-independent split. Reported: accuracy 95.82 %, precision 0.977, recall 0.97 (text and table); baselines in their table: customised 3D-CNN 86.7 % (the text says 87.7 %), VGGFace+LSTM 56.0 %. Adding demographic attributes lowered accuracy to 94.00 %. Their Grad-CAM discussion notes that participants showed prolonged eye closure, downward gaze or head tilting, movements resembling intoxication cues, and that false negatives occurred for intoxicated people with neutral expressions. Limits: YouTube data (not a controlled study), sensitivity to behavioural ambiguity and demographics; trained on a GTX TITAN X GPU. Closest published work to the batch title.

**[3] Deuber et al., arXiv:2605.23663, 22 May 2026. Verified from PDF.** Drunk-driving detection from a consumer smartwatch: wrist accelerometer plus heart-rate-variability-derived arousal signals; logistic regression with window features and a two-tower 1D CNN. Randomised, controlled three-arm test-track study with 54 participants (31 alcohol, 12 placebo, 11 open-label reference), three driving conditions (sober, above 0.05 g/dL, below 0.05 g/dL), leave-one-subject-out validation. Reported: participant-averaged AUROC 0.88 for any alcohol and 0.86 for driving above the WHO-recommended limit of 0.05 g/dL. The manuscript states it is submitted to ACM and under review (cite as preprint).

## Theme 2 - Intoxication detection from the face, thermal imaging and the vehicle

**[4] Mehta, Yadav, Katta, Dhall, "DIF: Dataset of Perceived Intoxicated Faces for Drunk Person Identification", arXiv:1805.10030 (v1 May 2018, v3 revised Sep 2019). Verified on the web (arXiv record and search summaries; authors corrected from my first draft).** Audio-visual dataset of intoxicated and sober people gathered from online sources; CNN (video) and DNN (audio) baselines; a 3D-convolution variant. v1 described RGB face videos with an LSTM over eye gaze, face pose and expression and found gaze and expression features discriminative. "Perceived" intoxication: labels come from how people look, not measured blood alcohol. Per [2], many source videos are no longer accessible.

**[5] L. P. Acharya, "Infrared Deep Learning System for Non-Invasive Drunk Detection and Alcohol Level Classification", Iowa State University repository record, Dec 2024 (advisor A. Dorgandzic). Abstract only (web search summary of the repository record; the page itself returned HTTP 403).** FLIR ONE thermal camera on an iPhone; 50 volunteers at four drinking levels (sober, one, two, three glasses); augmentation to more than 1,200 images. Reported: NasNetMobile 85.10 % on the four-level task, MobileNet 74.07 % on the binary sober-versus-drunk task. Limits stated: difficulty separating neighbouring consumption levels and accounting for body temperature changes. This is the batch survey item "Infrared Deep Learning System..., 2024"; it is a student repository record, not a journal paper.

**[6] F. Deiana, J. Jackson, C. Periago (Applus IDIADA), "Development and Verification of a Testing Methodology for Driver Alcohol Intoxication Detection Systems", AHFE IHSED2026 (Human Systems Engineering and Design: Future Trends and Applications), doi 10.54941/ahfe1008145. Verified on the AHFE open-access page (abstract).** A testing framework for driver monitoring systems that detect alcohol intoxication, motivated by Euro NCAP and regulators; needs data from participants given alcohol, which raises safety and ethics issues; piloted at the IDIADA proving ground in Spain with controlled intoxication protocols, vehicle instrumentation and safety measures; aims at a standardised benchmark so solutions can be compared. The abstract gives no numbers (no participant count, no BAC levels). The search summary listed the authors in a different order (Jackson first); the publisher page order is used.

**[7] "Alcohol Detection and Vehicle Immobilization System for Enhanced Taxi Driver Safety and Drunk Driving Prevention", ICOEI 2026. TITLE ONLY.** Two web searches (including extended mode) found no listing of this paper. The only thing that can be said is what its title says: breath-alcohol-style detection with vehicle immobilisation for taxi drivers. Do not quote any result, method or hardware. (Search turned up unrelated alcohol-interlock papers; they are not this entry and are not cited.)

**[8] C.-H. Chai, S. F. Abdul Razak, S. Yogarayan, R. Shanmugam, "Drunk Driver Detection Using Thermal Facial Images", Information (MDPI) 16(5):413, 2025. Verified on the web (abstract via search; MDPI page returned 403 to the fetch tool).** Thermal facial patterns for sober versus intoxicated classification; thermal images from the Tufts Face Database plus self-collected images; CNN and YOLO for face/feature extraction; SVM, MLP, KNN, Random Forest and linear regression classifiers; MLP about 90 % accuracy and best. Limits: only abstract and introduction read; test protocol not verified; thermal cameras are not in ordinary vehicles or laptops.

## Theme 3 - Eye measures, drowsiness and driver monitoring

**[9] T. Soukupova and J. Cech, "Real-time eye blink detection using facial landmarks", 21st Computer Vision Winter Workshop, Rimske Toplice, Slovenia, 3-5 Feb 2016. Verified on the web (workshop proceedings, CMP CTU).** Introduces the eye aspect ratio from six eye landmarks; blinks classified from a short window of EAR values with an SVM or by an HMM with a state machine; results comparable with the state of the art on three datasets.

**[10] Kartynnik, Ablavatski, Grishchenko, Grundmann, "Real-time facial surface geometry from monocular video on mobile GPUs", arXiv:1907.06724, CVPR Workshop on Computer Vision for AR/VR 2019. Verified on the web.** 468-vertex face mesh from one camera, 100 to 1000+ FPS on mobile GPUs depending on device and model variant; accuracy close to the spread between human annotators. **[11]** C. Lugaresi et al. (14 authors, Google Research), "MediaPipe: A framework for building perception pipelines", arXiv:1906.08172, 2019. Verified on the web (author list). **[24]** Google AI Edge, Face Landmarker guide for Python (URL named in the project README; not re-fetched).

**[12] Song, Tan, Liu, Chen, "Eyes closeness detection from still images with multi-scale histograms of principal oriented gradients", Pattern Recognition 47(9):2825-2838, Sep 2014, doi 10.1016/j.patcog.2014.03.024. Verified on the web (Crossref and the NUAA dataset page).** The CEW dataset: 2,423 subjects, 1,192 with both eyes closed collected from the Internet and 1,231 with eyes open selected from LFW [13]; provided as original-resolution faces, 100x100 warped faces and 24x24 eye patches. **[13]** G. B. Huang, M. Ramesh, T. Berg, E. Learned-Miller, "Labeled Faces in the Wild", UMass Amherst Tech. Rep. 07-49, Oct 2007: 13,233 images of 250x250 pixels in natural variation of pose, lighting, focus and expression. Verified on the web.

**[14] C.-H. Weng, Y.-H. Lai, S.-H. Lai, "Driver drowsiness detection via a hierarchical temporal deep belief network", ACCV 2016 Workshops (LNCS), Springer 2017, pp. 117-133, doi 10.1007/978-3-319-54526-4_9. Verified on the web (Crossref).** NTHU Driver Drowsiness Detection dataset, the data used by the base paper. Not read in full; only bibliographic data used.

**[15] FMCSA/FHWA Tech Brief FHWA-MCRT-98-006, "PERCLOS: A valid psychophysiological measure of alertness as assessed by psychomotor vigilance", Oct 1998. Verified on the web (ROSAP record).** The record lists the corporate creator, not individual authors (the earlier draft's "Dinges and Grace" could not be confirmed and was removed). PERCLOS measures eyelid closure over time, focusing on slow droops rather than blinks; defined in a 1994 driving-simulator study as the share of time within a minute that the eyes are at least 80 % closed; FHWA and NHTSA considered it among the most promising real-time alertness measures. Smart Rider's "closed fraction" is similar in spirit but uses a threshold on EAR and a 10 s window, and is explicitly not claimed to be PERCLOS.

**[16] J. A. Stern, D. Boyer, D. Schroeder, "Blink rate: a possible measure of fatigue", Human Factors 36(2):285-297, 1994, doi 10.1177/001872089403600209. Verified on the web (Crossref).** The related FAA report (DOT/FAA/AM-94/17, "Blink rate as a measure of fatigue: a review") states that blink rate rises with time on task and that closure duration may also be affected. Used for the blink-rate display in the system (not used for warnings).

**[27] Abtahi et al., "YawDD: A yawning detection dataset", ACM MMSys 2014, pp. 24-28, doi 10.1145/2557642.2563678. Verified on the web (Crossref).** Driver yawning video dataset; cited as an example of driver-state datasets that exist for drowsiness but not for intoxication. **[28]** Q. Ji, Z. Zhu, P. Lan, "Real-time nonintrusive monitoring and prediction of driver fatigue", IEEE Trans. Veh. Technol. 53(4):1052-1068, Jul 2004, doi 10.1109/TVT.2004.830974. Verified on the web (Crossref and the IEEE Xplore abstract: real-time prototype with infrared-illuminated cameras that combines visual cues of eyelid movement, gaze, head movement and facial expression with a probabilistic model; robust across subjects, glasses and lighting per the abstract). Full text not read.

**[29] Radford et al., "Robust speech recognition via large-scale weak supervision" (Whisper), ICML 2023, PMLR 202:28492-28518; trained on 680,000 hours. Verified on the web (PMLR).** Cited for the base paper's voice module only. **[30]** J.-B. Yang, J. Liu, J. Wang, H.-S. Sii, H.-W. Wang, "Belief rule-base inference methodology using the evidential reasoning approach - RIMER", IEEE Trans. SMC-A 36(2):266-285, Mar 2006, doi 10.1109/TSMCA.2005.851270. Verified on the web (Crossref). Cited for the base paper's reasoning method.

## Theme 4 - Road-safety context and testing practice

**[17] WHO, "Global status report on road safety 2023", Dec 2023. Verified on the web (WHO news release of 13 Dec 2023).** About 1.19 million road traffic deaths a year, a fall of 5 % since 2010 (16 % after adjusting for population growth). Riders of powered two- and three-wheelers such as motorcycles account for 21 % of global road traffic deaths, pedestrians 23 % (274,000), cyclists 6 % (71,000). The release says only six countries have laws that meet WHO best practice for all risk factors (speeding, drink-driving, motorcycle helmets, ...), and about 140 have such laws for at least one. It gives no separate number for alcohol-related deaths: do not attribute one to WHO.

**[18] NHTSA, "DWI Detection and Standardized Field Sobriety Testing (SFST) Participant Manual", 2023 edition. Verified on the web (NHTSA document page; content details not read).** The standardised police tests (Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus, Walk-and-Turn, One-Leg Stand) are validated indicators of impairment from NHTSA research; used only to show that eye behaviour is already part of how impairment is judged by trained officers.

## Facts about the base paper used in the comparison (REPORT_FACTS section 8)

* Eye feature: EAR-4 (four upper-eyelid points) against six-point EAR; head pose by PnP with three angles; voice with Whisper-tiny; fuzzy belief rule-based fusion; Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB at 25 FPS; NTHU-DDD four subjects, 65,834 frames; metrics on Subject 2 Random Forest: recall 0.8560 -> 0.8940, F1 0.8130 -> 0.8452.
* Smart Rider differences: six-point EAR only; image-plane roll only (not full 3D pose); no voice; transparent duration rules; laptop CPU; CEW still images for the EAR component; no rider dataset.
