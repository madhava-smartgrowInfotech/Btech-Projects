Reservation statuses: reserved, active, completed, cancelled, failed, declined. Payment statuses: pending, paid, refunded, failed.
- Core rules: admission `overlapping_bookings + buffer < capacity` (engine.forecast_free); buffer init 1, +1 on every ON-TIME checkout (<= end + 10 min) up to 5, reset to 1 every 24 h by the scheduler; arrival check `active_parked >= capacity` -> exactly one nearest alternate by haversine (R = 6371 km) with forecast space, auto-created as an active booking with parent_id, else status failed and fee credited; trust 0-5 start 3, 3 benefits -> +1, 2 warnings -> -1; fee multiplier {0:1.75,1:1.5,2:1.25,3:1.0,4:0.95,5:0.9}; minimum billed time 0.25 h; suspension when trust < 1.5 and a warning within 14 days (24 h cooldown, re-applied at each refused request); bookings limited to 30 days; account credit offsets fees.
- **Direction of the shipped dynamic buffer differs from arXiv:2605.17650** (code grows the buffer on on-time checkouts, the paper on late departures); the experiments below measure both (`dynamic_parksure` and `dynamic_paper`). The product code was not changed during the documentation run.
- Lockout difference: the engine's suspension re-triggers at every request while trust < 1.5 and the last warning is < 14 days old, so a suspended driver is locked out for at least 14 days after the last warning (paper: one subsequent session).

## 7. Over-booking race (concurrency)

- 10 trials x 20 simultaneous reservation requests for a window with exactly 1 bookable bay: **1 of 10 trials accepted more than one** (max 2). Cause: admission check and insert are not atomic (no lock / transaction isolation on the count). Objective 'prevents double bookings' is therefore met sequentially but not under simultaneous requests.

Findings: (1) the shipped dynamic rule refuses the most reservations (buffer climbs to 5 because most checkouts are on time) and does not reduce No-park events meaningfully; (2) the paper-literal rule refuses fewer than the shipped one and similar to static 3, (3) 'No park' is rare in every policy (at most ~1.4 per ~1,000 requests) because the nearest-alternate step absorbs the overflow; (4) static 0 has the lowest refusals and highest utilisation but the most moves to alternates and alternate overflows; (5) the Trust lockout, not the buffer, is the largest source of refused requests once 25 % or more of the drivers are bad; (6) the paper's claim that the dynamic buffer gives the best trade-off is NOT reproduced for the shipped rule in this simulator, and the paper's own setting (SUMO, its parameters) was not available.
