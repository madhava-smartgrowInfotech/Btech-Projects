## 1. Base paper [1]

**F. Bagheri, D. Buscaldi, D. Reforgiato Recupero, "LLM-Assisted Reviewer Assignment via Auditable Expertise Matching", IEEE Access, vol. 14, pp. 127834-127863, 2026, doi 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3724622.**
Authors are at the University of Cagliari (Italy) and Sorbonne Paris Nord (France). Received 22 Jul 2026, accepted 13 Aug 2026, published 18 Aug 2026 (read from the PDF; 30 pages). Index terms: LLM, LLM agent, recommender systems, reviewer assignment, semantic web.

**Problem.** Reviewer assignment problem (RAP): match submissions to qualified reviewers under expertise coverage, capacity and conflict-of-interest constraints. The paper argues that *affinity construction* (the paper-reviewer score) is the central difficulty, and that dense embeddings are hard to audit.

**Method (what "auditable expertise matching" means).** LLMs are restricted to *keyword induction*; they never rank reviewers or decide. (a) A heterogeneous *ensemble of producer LLMs* each returns 6-12 one-to-three-word keyphrases as a strict JSON array for a paper view (title, abstract summary, full abstract) or for each prior publication of a reviewer; (b) a stronger *judge* LLM picks the single best candidate list for faithfulness, coverage, specificity and non-redundancy (it may not rewrite); (c) deterministic normalisation (lower-casing, trimming, lemmatisation with WordNet, de-duplication); (d) every stage stores artefacts (prompts, model ids, raw candidates, winning index, final keywords, score matrices, top-3 lists) as an audit trail. Producers named in the text include Qwen 3, Grok 4 and LLaMA 4 (Fig. 5 discussion); Qwen 3 and Grok 4 account for a large share of judge-selected lists. GPT-4o-mini appears only as a single-LLM baseline taken from the authors' earlier study.
Matchers compared: TF-IDF cosine, keyword-set Jaccard and their mean (`(Scos+SJac)/2`); SBERT bi-encoder (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, reviewer = mean-pooled publication embeddings, cosine); two-stage bi-encoder + cross-encoder (`cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`, top-3 re-ranked, hybrid `lambda*norm(s_ce)+(1-lambda)*norm(s_text)`); keyword-aware SBERT variants (dual similarity; IDF-weighted soft keyword coverage with `alpha(p) in [0.3, 0.7]`); global keyword knowledge-graph with Node2Vec (cosine, expanded node-Jaccard, edge-Jaccard); document-local graphs (d=64, walk length 10, 50 walks/node, window 5).

**Data.** Authors' Dataset: **663 papers from 85 computer-science conferences** (EasyChair), **1,989 assignments (about 3 reviewers per paper)**. Reviewers' Dataset: **524 unique reviewer profiles**, up to **20 most-cited publications each** from Semantic Scholar. Relevance labels = historical assignments. "Dataset will be released upon acceptance" (footnote) / "available upon reasonable request" (Section IV-A.3).

**Metrics and protocol.** MRR@3, P@3, MAP@3 (macro-averaged over papers); paired two-sided t-tests with 95 % CIs (bootstrap fallback, 10,000 resamples); workload diagnostics (per-conference mean, macro-average, pooled mean) and the **Gini coefficient** of reviewer load.

**Reported numbers (exact, MRR@3 / P@3 / MAP@3 unless stated).**
- Titles: Keyword-Aware Two-Stage best MRR@3 **0.7778**; Keyword-Aware SBERT best P@3 **0.4359** (MRR@3 0.6025, MAP@3 0.2992); Average Cosine+Jaccard best MAP@3 **0.2993**; TF-IDF cosine 0.7078/0.3882/0.2941; Jaccard 0.6892/0.3649/0.2639; SBERT (cosine) 0.6571/0.3873/0.2636; KG Edge Jaccard MRR@3 0.7556, P@3 0.3556.
- Summaries: SBERT 0.6858/0.3878/0.2772; Keyword-Aware SBERT 0.6212/0.4545/0.3232.
- Abstracts: KG Edge Jaccard best MRR@3 **0.7843** and MAP@3 **0.2991**; Keyword-Aware SBERT best P@3 **0.4000**; SBERT Text Similarity 0.7152/0.3958/0.2922; SBERT (cosine) 0.7023/0.3911/0.2857; Average Cosine+Jaccard 0.6379/0.3704/0.2476; TF-IDF 0.6235/0.3686/0.2435.
- Cross-encoder re-ranking on titles: MRR@3 +0.1173 (+17.76 %) over the keyword-aware bi-encoder, P@3 -0.0133.
- Significance: most differences among text encoders are statistically indistinguishable (e.g. titles, Keyword-Aware Bi-Encoder vs SBERT baseline: dP@3 = +0.0030, 95 % CI [-0.0022, 0.0082], p = 0.257); Local KG (Node2Vec-cosine) on titles significantly worse (dMAP@3 = -0.0414, CI [-0.0507, -0.0321]).
- Workload (Gini): SBERT baseline 0.375 (titles), 0.376 (summaries), 0.373 (abstracts); Average Cosine+Jaccard on titles 0.343; KG Edge Jaccard on summaries 0.539; Local KG (Node2Vec-cosine) on titles 0.680.
- Task context cited in the paper: NeurIPS 2021 repeat committees disagreed on about 23 % of submissions; 91 % of the top-100 medical journals prohibit uploading manuscripts to AI tools, 59 % prohibit AI in peer review.

**Limitations stated by the authors.** Historical assignments are an imperfect proxy for expertise (availability, bidding, COI and workload also shaped them); no human expert judgements; no capacity/COI-aware end-to-end allocation was benchmarked (COI and capacity metadata unavailable); LLM keywords may hallucinate, drift between model versions and carry bias; stored artefacts do not allow a producer-by-producer ablation; the ensemble-and-judge advantage over a single LLM is not established; adversarial manipulation defences are future work.

**What it does not do (relevant to the gap).** It matches reviewers only - there is *no assessment of the submission itself* (structure, evidence, clarity) and no reviewer-facing UI; its scoring is offline over a fixed dataset; availability and COI are left to a downstream solver.

**How PeerScope differs (precise).** PeerScope [code in `app/services/matching.py`] adopts the *auditable* principle - every recommendation exposes its score breakdown (`0.55*expertise + 0.20*keyword + 0.15*method + 0.10*availability - 0.60*conflict penalty`, 0-100) - but (1) it does **not** use an LLM ensemble/judge for keywords: keywords and method terms come from deterministic frequency/lexicon extraction (`app/services/features.py`), and the local Qwen2.5 model is used only for the *review text*, never for ranking; (2) its expertise similarity is a single bi-encoder cosine (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, the same encoder the base paper uses for SBERT, falling back to TF-IDF); (3) it adds *availability* (current/maximum load) and a *conflict penalty* inside the score instead of leaving them to an external solver, and does not run a global optimiser (ranking only); (4) it adds what the base paper lacks: PDF intake, preliminary paper assessment (structural signals, trained decision signal, OpenAlex literature grounding), a reviewer directory with CRUD/CSV, history and export. It was evaluated on a small demo reviewer pool, not on 663 papers / 524 reviewers, so its numbers are not comparable with the paper's MRR/MAP.

## 2. Supporting papers archived with the batch

- **[6] Wu et al., arXiv:2604.27924 (30 Apr 2026)** - survey from Yale / NYU / TCS Research of LLM-assisted peer review across *review generation* (fine-tuning, agents, RL), *after-review tasks* (rebuttal, meta-review, revision) and *evaluation* (human-centred, reference-based, LLM-based, aspect-oriented); catalogues datasets, discusses ethics and limits. No new system or measurements (secondary source). It does not treat reviewer assignment as its main topic.
- **[7] Li et al., ReViewGraph, arXiv:2511.08317 (Nov 2025; AAAI 2026 copyright line)** - simulates multi-round reviewer-author debates with LLM agents, builds a heterogeneous graph of typed opinion edges and applies a GNN to predict accept/reject. Data: ICLR 2023/2024/2025 submissions crawled from OpenReview (train/val/test sizes in its Table 1, e.g. 2025 test = 388 papers). Reported accuracy / macro-F1: ICLR 2023 70.29 / 69.89, ICLR 2024 66.10 / 65.54, ICLR 2025 71.65 / 71.65; "average relative improvement of 15.73 %" over the second-best of 7 baselines. Limitation: decisions only, no reviewer matching, needs expensive multi-agent LLM runs.

## 3. Batch literature survey items [2]-[5]

- **[2] Torrielli, Locci, Rapp, Di Caro (Research Square preprint, 5 Jan 2026; journal version Scientometrics 131(7):4889-4951, 2026).** Author-Reviewer-Organizer framework; indirect prompt injection (hidden instructions in a manuscript). Preprint abstract: 5,600 controlled experiments on NeurIPS/ICLR manuscripts, two general-purpose chatbots, hidden instructions followed in **78 % (ChatGPT) and 86 % (Gemini)** of cases; early-position payloads are the most influential. Journal abstract: 100 OpenReview papers, 42,000 outputs, positive steering/refusal/redirection above 98 % success. Relevance: an AI-assisted reviewer that reads uploaded text can be manipulated - motivates PeerScope's "decision support only", local processing and visible disclaimers.
- **[3] Sermet and Demir, ScienceOpen, 14 May 2025.** Title matches the batch survey; the Crossref record carries no abstract and the full text was not read - **content unverified beyond title, authors and date**. Cited only as an evidence-based study of how reviewers behave, as the batch survey lists it.
- **[4], [5] Peer Community in Evolutionary Biology review reports, Round#1/Reviewer#2 (26 Jun 2024) and Round#2/Reviewer#2 (1 Dec 2024)** for Romieu et al., "Performance evaluation of adaptive introgression classification methods" (Peer Community Journal vol. 5, e95, 2025, doi 10.24072/pcjournal.617; 6 authors: Romieu, Camarata, Crochet, de Navascues, Leblois, Rousset). ## 4. Related work, with verified facts

Reviewer assignment
- **[8] Jovanovic & Bagheri (2023)** - scoping review; 26 studies retained from Scopus, Google Scholar and DBLP; notes RAP has been studied for about thirty years and that a recent systematic synthesis was missing.
- **[9] Zhao & Zhang (IPM 2022)** - survey of reviewer-assignment algorithms 1992-2022 organised as candidate-reviewer database, matching-degree computation, assignment optimisation; also summarises metrics and datasets.
- **[10] Charlin, Zemel, Boutilier (2012)** - suitability scores between papers and reviewers learned from few provided scores, then an integer program for assignment; tested on two conference datasets. (The base paper's TPMS reference, a 2013 technical report, was not locatable; this related paper of the same group is cited.)
- **[11] Mimno & McCallum (KDD 2007)** - compares two language-model approaches with a novel Author-Persona-Topic model; evaluated against human relevance judgements of reviewer suitability on documents from the Rexa database; APT outperformed the others.
- **[12] Stelmakh, Shah, Singh (2018/ALT 2019)** - PeerReview4All: incremental max-flow assignment that maximises quality for the *most disadvantaged* paper; minimax accuracy analysis; a new experiment design for comparing assignment algorithms without ground truth.
- **[13] Beygelzimer et al. (2023)** - NeurIPS 2021 consistency experiment: two independent committees reviewed 10 % of submissions and **disagreed on 23 %** of accept/reject outcomes; about half the accepted list would change on re-run.
- **[14] Bouanane et al. (IPM 2024) FairColor** - balanced and fair reviewer-assignment algorithm; bibliographic record verified only, abstract not read.
- **[15] Jecmen et al. (2020)** - randomised reviewer assignment to limit malicious reviewers (quid-pro-quo), "torpedo reviewing" and de-anonymisation; NP-hard for arbitrary joint-probability constraints, efficient for a practical special case; tested on past conference data.
- **[16] Zhang et al., Chain-of-Factors (WWW 2025)** - one instruction-tuned language model chains semantic, topic and citation factors for coarse-to-fine reviewer search; experiments on four datasets (one new). Limitation: single-factor shortcuts of earlier work are what it fixes; needs training.

Text representation
- **[17] Salton & Buckley (1988)** - term-weighting approaches (TF-IDF family) for retrieval. **[18] Sparck Jones (1972)** - inverse document frequency. Used here for the baseline TF-IDF vectoriser/classifier and the TF-IDF fallback of the matcher.
- **[19] Reimers & Gurevych (EMNLP 2019) SBERT** - siamese BERT giving embeddings comparable by cosine; reduces finding the most similar pair among 10,000 sentences from about 65 hours (BERT) to about 5 seconds. **[20] Wang et al. (2020) MiniLM** - deep self-attention distillation, the compact family to which `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` belongs (the exact model card was not read; the base paper names that checkpoint). **[21] Cohan et al. (ACL 2020) SPECTER** - citation-informed document embeddings for scientific documents; evaluated on the SciDocs benchmark of seven tasks.

Automated review and decision prediction
- **[22] Kang et al. (NAACL 2018) PeerRead** - 14.7K paper drafts with accept/reject decisions (ACL, NIPS, ICLR) and 10.7K textual reviews; simple models predict acceptance with up to **21 % error reduction** over the majority baseline.
- **[23] Yuan, Liu, Neubig (JAIR 2022)** - first-pass review generation with summarisation models; generated reviews cover more aspects than human ones but are less constructive.
- **[24] Liang et al. (NEJM AI 2024)** - GPT-4 feedback on full PDFs: overlap with human reviewers 30.85 % (Nature journals, 3,096 papers) and 39.23 % (ICLR, 1,709 papers) vs 28.58 % / 35.25 % between two humans; user study with 308 researchers: 57.4 % found it helpful or very helpful, 82.4 % found it more beneficial than at least some human reviews; limitations noted.
- **[25] Zhu et al., DeepReview (ACL 2025)** - multi-stage review with literature retrieval and evidence-based argumentation; DeepReviewer-14B trained on DeepReview-13K, win rate 88.21 % vs GPT-o1 and 80.20 % vs DeepSeek-R1.
- **[26] Zhuang et al. (Inf. Fusion 2025)** - survey of LLMs for automated paper review (record verified; abstract not read).

Integrity and tooling
- **[27] Keuper (2025)** - 1k ICLR 2024 reviews generated by several LLMs: very simple prompt injections reach up to 100 % acceptance scores; LLM reviews are biased toward acceptance (>95 % for many models).
- **[28] Vaswani et al. (2017)**, **[29] Qwen2.5 Technical Report (2024)** - pre-training scaled from 7 to 18 trillion tokens; open-weight instruct models are the family PeerScope loads (`Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct`, fallback `0.5B`). **[30] Wolf et al. (EMNLP demos 2020)** - the `transformers` library used to load it. **[31] Pedregosa et al.** - scikit-learn (TF-IDF, LogisticRegression, metrics). **[32] Priem et al. (2022)** - OpenAlex: 209 M works, free REST API, the source of PeerScope's literature-grounding step.

## 5. Research-gap statements (grounded above)

1. The base paper [1] improves and audits the *affinity matrix* only: it scores 663 papers against 524 reviewers but performs **no assessment of the paper itself**, so editors still need a separate tool for first-pass screening [1], [6].
2. Automated review work [22]-[25], [7] predicts decisions or writes reviews but **does not recommend reviewers**; the two halves of the editorial workload are served by disjoint research lines.
3. The base paper's own evaluation relies on historical assignments as labels and leaves **conflict-of-interest and capacity out of the experiments** (metadata unavailable) [1], so how availability and COI should enter an *auditable score* is untested; [15] treats manipulation/COI by randomisation, not by a transparent per-reviewer score.
4. LLM-based reviewing is **vulnerable to hidden instructions** (78-86 % compliance [2]; up to 100 % acceptance scores [27]) and biased toward acceptance, which argues for local processing, a deterministic fallback and human-in-the-loop framing rather than autonomous LLM verdicts.
5. Dense-embedding matchers are **hard to audit** [1]; per-recommendation score breakdowns with matched terms and explicit availability/conflict terms are rare in student-deployable systems, and none of [10]-[16] ships as an end-to-end local application with PDF intake.
6. Published decision-prediction results depend on real review corpora [22], [7]; a project without access to such data must state plainly that a classifier trained on **synthetic** labelled text demonstrates the pipeline, not real-world predictive power (this is the position taken for PeerScope's `models/metrics.json`: `synthetic_fallback: true`).
